Scan Sync

Mirror every scan on this device to your own TSG Biom server, so real scans can be reviewed off-device. Each scan posts a small record (who, which hand, the scores, the result) and each palm-bank change posts the palm codes — never a palm image. From v61 the codes are sealed on this device under its Biom Vault key before they are stored or posted, once the vault is set up (More → Biom Vault); a bank the vault has not sealed yet posts in its encoded form, and the vault card above shows which banks are sealed. Matching always stays on this device; sync runs in the background and never delays a scan. On by default from v63 — it can be switched off below, by the owner only.

From v63 this device registers itself automatically: the app carries a built-in registration key whose only server power is registering this device. The server then issues this device its own credential (below), every write is signed with it, and it can be revoked on its own if this device is ever lost. The registration key cannot read or write anyone's palm banks. Bank writes are version-checked on the server, so a stale device can no longer overwrite a newer bank.

This build connects only to the TSG Biom server above — the endpoint is fixed for this build, so every device syncs to the same vault.

How the real scanning works

Palm only. MediaPipe landmarks locate the palm; a 96×96 patch is cut out and aligned from the wrist and knuckle anchors.

Real matching. An EDCC palmprint encoder reads the palm's own print pattern, and verification scores the live patch against the enrolled templates — the best match decides.

Nothing is simulated. If no hand or no clear print is seen, the scan fails openly. Palm-vein scanning needs infrared hardware, which a normal camera does not have — we do not fake it.

How the palm scan works, in full

The camera opens when this step opens — nothing is captured until you tap Scan Palm. Position your open hand in the frame, anywhere in view, at a natural distance — there is nothing to line up: the app finds the hand by itself. On the tap, the app watches live, and the moment a sharp open palm is seen, a single 900 ms capture burst runs, and the app banks the three sharpest time-spaced templates from it. Liveness is checked across the burst: a real hand drifts naturally by a tiny amount and its landmarks carry real depth variance — a photo or a screen cannot, so a single photo is never accepted. A palm pressed right against the lens cannot be read, so keep a natural distance. The palm square is cut out and aligned from the hand landmarks, and an EDCC palmprint encoder reads the palm's own print pattern — the templates are stored bit-packed on this device. Labelled honestly: this is Palm — Palmprint, the print-pattern method real palmprint systems use. Palm-vein scanning needs infrared hardware, which a normal camera does not have — we do not fake it.

Partner Integration

Biom Pay authorizes a payment biometrically on this terminal. The bKash rail is connected in SANDBOX test mode through the TSG Biom API (app v71): a palm-authorised wallet payment can be initiated into bKash's sandbox, where the customer completes the ceremony on bKash's own page — no real money moves in the sandbox. Production money moves only through the official bKash / Nagad merchant APIs, after TwoSide Group signs the collaboration and merchant credentials are configured on the API server. Until then, non-sandbox transactions are recorded truthfully as biometric-authorized, pending partner settlement — TSG Biom never invents a bKash / Nagad transaction ID.
