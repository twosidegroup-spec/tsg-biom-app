How the real scanning works

Face: a real face-recognition model extracts a 128-point descriptor; verification measures the actual distance between scans. Palm: MediaPipe landmarks locate the palm, a square palm image is cut out and aligned, and an EDCC palmprint encoder reads the palm's own print pattern — the method family used by real palmprint systems. Fingerprint: a camera photographs your fingertip and the system enhances and matches the real ridge pattern from those pixels. Nothing here is simulated — if no face, hand or clear print is seen, the scan fails openly. Enroll any one, two, or all three - each biometric works independently.

Step 3 — Palm scan (real palmprint)

Palmprint enrollment from a short recorded clip: tap Record and, during the 4 seconds, open the palm wide, close it into a loose fist, then open it wide again and hold it open — a photo cannot do this, and a single photo is never accepted. Hold the phone about 30 cm away so the whole hand fits inside the frame; a palm pressed close to the lens cannot be read. The clip is analysed frame by frame: the movement is checked, the palm square is cut out and aligned from the hand landmarks, and an EDCC palmprint encoder reads the palm's own print pattern from the best open-palm frames — three print codes are stored. Labelled honestly: this is Palm — Palmprint, the print-pattern method real palmprint systems use. Palm-vein scanning needs infrared hardware, which a normal camera does not have — we do not fake it.

Front camera = self-scan. Back camera = a cashier scans a customer across the counter. This choice applies to this step only.

Step 4 — Fingerprint through the camera (real)

Defaults to the back camera — you can switch to the front camera with the toggle below. Hold your fingertip facing the camera, 5–8 cm away, filling the guide, in good light, and keep still. Turn Flash On if the fingertip is too dark - flash is manual, it never comes on by itself. You will see your original photo and the enhanced ridge image taken from its real pixels. Blurry prints are rejected — never invented.
